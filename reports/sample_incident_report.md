## Findings

### ALT-1004 — Malware hash detected (Critical)

- **What happened:** `payroll-update.exe` on workstation-14
  (user anita.desai) matched a SHA-256 hash seen in a recent
  malware report. The endpoint agent quarantined the file
  automatically at 06:11:55 and blocked it from running.
- **Evidence:** `auth.log` — "QUARANTINED payroll-update.exe
  sha256=e3b0c44298fc…" and "blocked from execution".
- **Risk:** High if the file had run; as quarantined, the immediate
  risk is contained, but the workstation may have other payloads.
- **Recommended next step:** Keep the file in quarantine, run a full
  scan on workstation-14, check what downloaded the file, and ask
  anita.desai how she received it. Do not restore the file.

### ALT-1001 — SSH brute-force, then a successful login (High)

- **What happened:** 142 failed SSH logins for `root` on web-01
  from 203.0.113.45 in 10 minutes, followed by one successful login
  at 02:14:07 from the same IP.
- **Evidence:** `auth.log` shows repeated "Failed password for root
  from 203.0.113.45" and then "Accepted password for root from
  203.0.113.45". An IP lookup places 203.0.113.45 outside the
  company's usual regions.
- **Risk:** This is the most urgent alert after the malware one —
  a successful login after a brute-force almost always means the
  attacker is inside.
- **Recommended next step:** Treat web-01 as compromised until
  proven otherwise: disable the `root` password login, force a
  password reset, block 203.0.113.45 at the firewall, and check
  what the root session did after 02:14.

### ALT-1002 — Phishing email (High)

- **What happened:** An email from
  `accounts-update@example-billing.com` ("Overdue invoice #INV-8841")
  linked to a credential-harvesting page. The recipient clicked
  the link once before reporting it.
- **Evidence:** Mail-gateway log shows the message arriving at
  03:02:41 for priya.sharma@example.com. The sender domain does
  not match any real billing system used by the company.
- **Risk:** The click may have exposed credentials if the user typed
  them in on the fake page.
- **Recommended next step:** Ask priya.sharma whether she entered
  any password on that page; if yes, reset it at once. Block the
  sender domain and the link, and warn the team about this invoice
  lure.

### ALT-1003 — Suspicious login from a new country (Medium)

- **What happened:** rahul.verma logged in via VPN from his usual
  city at 04:27, then again at 04:47 from a country he has never
  logged in from — 20 minutes apart, which is impossible travel.
- **Evidence:** VPN log lines at 04:27:03 and 04:47:19, plus the
  gateway's own "impossible-travel" warning. An IP lookup for
  198.51.100.23 confirms a different country from his usual login.
- **Risk:** Medium — this pattern fits a stolen session or a
  shared credential, but it could also be a VPN exit node.
- **Recommended next step:** Ask rahul.verma if the 04:47 login was
  his. If he says no, reset his password, end all sessions, and
  turn on stricter MFA for VPN logins.

### ALT-1005 — Port scan (Medium)

- **What happened:** 203.0.113.78 probed 48 ports on the edge
  firewall in 3 minutes (22, 80, 443, 3306, 3389 and others).
- **Evidence:** Firewall log shows every probe as `[UFW BLOCK]`
  from 203.0.113.78. No port was open to the scanner.
- **Risk:** Low right now — this is reconnaissance, not a breach.
  It is worth watching because the same network range also
  produced the SSH brute-force in ALT-1001.
- **Recommended next step:** Block 203.0.113.78 at the edge,
  keep watching that range, and check whether any other host
  scanned the same ports this week.

### ALT-1006 — Failed MFA (Low)

- **What happened:** karan.mehta failed MFA push approval 3 times,
  then succeeded on the 4th attempt from his usual device and city.
- **Evidence:** SSO log shows three "MFA push denied" lines and
  then "MFA push approved … (usual device, usual city)".
- **Risk:** Low — three failed pushes followed by a success from
  the usual device looks like a user tapping the wrong button
  (or approving late), not an attacker.
- **Recommended next step:** No action needed. If failed MFA
  attempts for this user keep repeating, ask him about it.
